<SectionCard>

**Batchim** (받침) means the consonant that sits at the bottom of a syllable block. You'll see a letter behave one way at the start and sound different at the end.

The word literally means *support* or *prop*, which makes sense—this consonant is literally holding up the rest of the block.

Pronunciation: the final consonant in **받** isn't a hard English *d*. It ends short and unreleased, so it sounds closer to *bat-chim* than *bad-chim*.

Three essential rules to memorize: final stops are short, final ㅇ becomes *ng*, and final ㄹ becomes *l*. That's it for day one.

</SectionCard>

## How to think about it

<ExampleGrid cols={3}>

<SectionCard title="Top vs bottom">

The same consonant can behave one way at the start of a block and another way at the bottom. Batchim means "bottom position".

</SectionCard>

<SectionCard title="Short ending sound">

Batchim sounds cut off fast. Don't add an extra vowel after them.

</SectionCard>

<SectionCard title="Learn a few patterns first">

Skip the exceptions at first. Start with final ng, final l, and short stop endings.

</SectionCard>

</ExampleGrid>

## First rules to remember

<ExampleGrid cols={3}>

<SectionCard title="Stop finals">

ㄱ ㄷ ㅂ-family finals end short and unreleased. Do not add a big vowel after them.

</SectionCard>

<SectionCard title="ㅇ final">

At the bottom of a block, ㅇ sounds like ng.

</SectionCard>

<SectionCard title="ㄹ final">

At the bottom of a block, ㄹ is an l-sound.

</SectionCard>

</ExampleGrid>

## The 7 final sounds

Many consonants can sit at the bottom of a block. At the end of a word, they simplify into **7 basic sounds**.
